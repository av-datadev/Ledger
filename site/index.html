The problem

A house isn't one payment. It's a thousand.

Spread over three or four years, paid by different people in the family, in cash and UPI and cheques, against bills that are half printed invoices and half notebook pages in Hindi. By the second year nobody can say what the plumbing has actually cost.

  • The WhatsApp group where the numbers live is not a ledger, and cannot be added up.
  • A kaccha bill records what was paid and what is still owed — on the same sheet of paper.
  • Material handed to a plumber on Tuesday is nobody's record by Friday.
  • The site has no signal, and the person holding the numbers isn't at a desk.
“Every figure was in somebody's head, somebody's diary, or somebody's chat. Getting one straight answer meant three phone calls.” Why this app exists
How it works

Snap it. Say it. See it.

Three ways in, because a person standing on a site with dusty hands is not going to fill in a form.

01 — SNAP IT

Photograph the bill

A printed GST invoice, a PDF, or a handwritten kaccha bill in Hindi — it comes back as editable line items, with the totals and what's still owed. A bill spread over three notebook pages reads as one bill. The photo stays attached as proof.

02 — SAY IT

Or just say it out loud

Hold the microphone and speak in Hindi, English, or the mix everyone actually uses. The form below fills itself, and shows you exactly what it heard before anything is saved.

03 — SEE IT

The whole picture, instantly

Spend by category, what each person has been paid, what's been bought against what's been used, and the gap between money paid and bills accounted for. Tap any figure to see the payments behind it.

Two sides, one app

Whether you're building it, or building for someone.

The person paying for a house and the person running the site keep different books, and want different things from them. Brick Book is both — you pick your side the first time you open it.

If you're building a home

Your money, your materials, your people.

One ledger the whole family shares — so your father recording a cash payment in the morning shows up on your phone before lunch, and nobody has to be asked twice.

  • Ledger & dashboard — every payment by category, mode and who paid, with a budget bar and a running total.
  • Bills (BOQ) — scanned or typed, grouped into one running account per dealer, showing what each is still owed.
  • Stock — what came in on which bill, what went out to whom, and what's left on the shelf.
  • People — each trade and the person doing it, their contract price, and what they've been paid against it.
  • Paid vs billed — the money handed over that no bill accounts for yet, per payee.

If you're a contractor

Every site's hisaab, in your pocket.

Several sites at once, each with its own money log: what the owner gave you, what went out on material and labour, and what's left. No account needed — it works signed out, on your own phone.

  • A book per site — money received, material, labour and everything else, with a running balance.
  • Photograph the bill against the row — so the balance splits into spending you can prove and spending you can't.
  • Say the row instead of typing it — “aaj maalik se pachas hazaar liye” logs ₹50,000 received.
  • Share one site with the owner — he sees that site's rows and nothing else. Your other sites stay yours.
  • Back it up to your own account — a lost phone shouldn't take several families' books with it.

Details that matter

Built for how India actually builds.

Not a foreign expense tracker with rupees bolted on.

Kaccha bills, read properly

A notebook page in Devanagari with जमा and शेष on it becomes a bill, a payment, or both — you choose which.

It knows “dedh” and “dhai”

Saadhe teen hazaar is ₹3,500. Paune do lakh is ₹1,75,000. The way the number is actually said, not the way it's written.

Lakhs, not millions

₹48,62,400 — grouped the way you read it, everywhere, including on the way out to Excel.

Find the fitting, not the paper

Type “tee 1” and get Brass Tee 1" — never the 1.5 inch one. Search the bills and take a line straight into stock.

Timber priced by size

8¼ ft × 9 in × 8 in, three pieces — the cubic feet are worked out and checked against what the dealer wrote.

Bank details from a QR

Point the camera at a payee's UPI QR code and their account details fill themselves in.

Bring your old spreadsheet

Import the Excel file you've been keeping — the columns are worked out for you, and the total is checked against the one at the bottom of your sheet.

Your data, in your hands

Backup to a JSON or Excel file you keep yourself, CSV exports, and a project PDF for the bank or your CA.

Bigger text, when you need it

A text-size control for the person in the family who is actually going to be reading these numbers.

Privacy

Your records don't leave your phone unless you say so.

This is a book of what your family spent on your house. It's built to be held, not harvested.

Offline first, on-device by default

Everything is stored on your phone and works in airplane mode. Nothing is uploaded until you choose to sign in and share the book with your family.

The AI reader is opt-in, and says so first

Scanning a bill or speaking an entry sends that one photo or that one recording to be read, and the app tells you plainly before the first time. Both switch off, separately, in one tap. No recording is ever stored.

No ads, no analytics, no tracking

No advertising networks, no usage tracking, no data brokers. Your records are never sold, shared for marketing, or used to build a profile of you.

Delete it all, from inside the app

One control removes your account and everything held in the cloud for it. Your family's own books stay theirs.
