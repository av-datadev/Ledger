# BRICK BOOK — DESIGN CONSTITUTION v1.0

## Purpose
Brick Book is a construction-finance operating system for long-running house construction projects in India.

It must feel: solid, calm, trustworthy, financially precise, construction-specific, intelligent, practical, premium.

It must NOT feel like: generic SaaS, generic fintech, generic construction ERP, generic admin dashboard, Material Design clone, iOS Settings clone, template marketplace UI, or AI-generated dashboard.

## North-star principle
> Make complexity feel physically organized.

Brick Book deals with thousands of payments, bills, quantities, vendors, labour, stock movements and years of construction. Reduce cognitive load through hierarchy, grouping and progressive disclosure — not tiny typography.

## Visual laws
1. Hierarchy before decoration.
2. Surfaces have meaning; do not wrap every row/field in a card.
3. One dominant action per region.
4. Money is typography: financial values get deliberate scale, weight and alignment.
5. Metadata stays quiet.
6. Progressive disclosure is the default.
7. No arbitrary visual invention.
8. Mobile first, not mobile only.
9. Offline is a feature, not an implementation detail.
10. Construction specificity must survive even when the logo is hidden.

## Avoid
Excessive rounded cards, excessive borders, decorative gradients, glassmorphism, huge hero illustrations, generic blue fintech palettes, purple AI gradients, excessive shadows, tiny body text, repeated edit/delete buttons, giant empty dashboard cards, charts without decisions, modal overload, emoji as the primary icon system, and gratuitous animation.

## Information choreography
1. What matters?
2. What changed?
3. What needs attention?
4. What can I do?
5. What is the underlying detail?
6. What are the advanced controls?

## Signature Brick Book patterns
Financial trail: Payment ↔ Person/Vendor ↔ Bill ↔ BOQ item ↔ Stock.
Capture: Scan / Speak / Write → Extract → Review → Correct → Confirm → Saved.
Stock: Received − Given out = Remaining.
Contractor cash: Received − Spent = In-hand.
Contract: Contract value − Paid = Remaining.

## Quality test
Ask:
- Could this belong to another app?
- Is the most important number obvious in 2 seconds?
- Is the primary action obvious?
- Are there unnecessary cards?
- Is any text too small?
- Are metadata and controls quieter than the main information?
- Does the screen communicate state?
- Does it work offline?
- Does it remain usable with 1000+ records?
- Does it look deliberate rather than generated?

If the answer to "Could this belong to another app?" is yes, redesign it.
